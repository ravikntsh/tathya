# India Record: Interactive Site Requirements

Oct 16, 2026 · Ravi Sharma

A source-first, mobile-first site that shows India's record since 1857, what has improved, where it has lagged, and who did what, so every claim in a debate can be backed with one shareable link.

## Revision 2 (Oct 16, 2026)

Decisions made after an outside review (a ChatGPT-drafted "Product Blueprint"). Where this section conflicts with the rest of the document, this section wins.

**Purpose and audience**
- Debate support stays, but it isn't the whole purpose. The site is also for young Indians, including the Gen-Z protesters of 2026, to see what the situation is, how it changed and how it used to be, told with data, photos and plain language.
- Framing: "on India's side, not any party's". Don't label the site "nationalist".
- The "Lessons from the Threads arguments" and Myth vs record stay central.

**Photos and media (new section)**
- A photo and media section: then-and-now pairs, events and places, shown on the timeline and on topic pages.
- Photos come from two routes, both with a credit and licence on every image:
  - **Sourced online:** public domain, Creative Commons, or used with permission, with the original URL and licence recorded. A photo being on a government or news website doesn't make it free to use. No news-agency photos without a licence.
  - **Moderator uploads:** a moderator adds their own photo or one supplied by a contributor, with the photographer's name, date, place, and a declaration of rights.
- Every photo carries a caption, date, place and source. No AI-generated images of real people or events. Illustrations are labelled as illustrations.
- Photos go through the same two-approval review as other contributions.

**Border & neighbours, and India in the world (new pages)**
- **Border & neighbours** replaces "Border and China". It has one page per neighbour: China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Maldives and Afghanistan. Each page shows how the relationship has changed since 1947, through treaties, wars and clashes, trade, aid, migration, border agreements and incidents, all dated and sourced (MEA, treaties, Parliament answers, SIPRI, UN Comtrade).
- **India in the world** covers four areas:
  - How other countries see India: Pew Global Attitudes and other surveys with published methods, showing who was asked, when and how many.
  - GDP and economic rank: IMF, World Bank and Maddison, in both PPP and market-rate terms.
  - Military strength: SIPRI spending and arms transfers. Spending is an input, not proof of capability, and commercial "power rankings" are labelled with their method.
  - Diplomacy and trade: memberships, embassies and trade partners.
- The same rules apply as everywhere else: each source is its own line, every index carries a methodology note, and wins sit beside setbacks.

Topics are reached through broad, neutral sections, not through headings that read as accusations. A reader who feels attacked by a heading leaves before seeing the data. Every fact stays; only the way in changes.

**Who governed, party pages, current issues and chart notices (Oct 2026)**
- **Aim**, as the editor stated it: help young Indians, including the protesters of 2026, see where India started, how long change takes, and what each government did. We build it to the founding standard, "on India's side, not any party's": every party, including the current government, is judged by the same yardstick, so the facts persuade on their own.
- **Who governed:**
  - A sourced dataset of Union governments (Prime Minister, party, coalition, dates) and state Chief Ministers (party, dates, President's Rule).
  - The map can show the governing party per state, but **without colours**: a text label with the party abbreviation on each state. The fact card names the Chief Minister and the Union government at the time.
- **Party and coalition pages:**
  - One template for every party: national metrics while it led the government and while it was in opposition, its signature programmes (linked to the project ledger), its legislative record, and achievements and setbacks, each with a source.
  - Scandals are included only where there is a court judgment, a CAG or official inquiry finding, or the party's own admission. The legal status is shown separately from the evidence, and allegations are labelled as allegations.
- **Current issues:** each live protest or allegation gets a page showing the claim, what the site's data shows (linked charts), what is still unknown, and the legal status, with a dated update log.
- **Chart notices:** sudden changes in a chart are marked with dated context events (wars, droughts, the Emergency, 1991, the 2008 crisis, demonetisation, GST, COVID-19 and so on), each with a source. A marker explains the timing; it never claims a cause.

**National security (replaces "Terror" and "Insurgency")**
- **External:** cross-border terror attacks, infiltration, ceasefire violations, and wars and clashes.
- **Internal:** J&K militancy, Left-wing extremism and the North-East insurgencies.
- For each: incidents and deaths (civilians, security forces, militants as the source splits them) per year, plus the running total to date. Primary source: MHA annual reports and Parliament answers. Incident trackers (e.g. SATP) only as class E, cross-checked, with their definitions shown.

**Charts: compare two dates.** Two fingers on a chart (or click-and-drag on a computer) select two years, as in a phone stock app. The chart shades the range and shows each source's value at both ends and the change, both absolute and in %.

**Communal relations (renamed from "Communal violence")**
- The rail item and page are called "Communal relations". The page covers both incidents and coexistence:
  - Incidents, riots and deaths, labelled exactly as their sources label them (NCRB "communal/religious riots", MHA "communal incidents"), with reporting caveats.
  - Survey data on attitudes and coexistence (for example, Pew Research Center's 2021 "Religion in India: Tolerance and Segregation"), with sample and method notes.
- The heading is neutral, so it neither hides incidents nor shows only the bad side.

**Login**
- Sign in with Google, or with email and password. No magic links, and no phone OTP.

**Evidence model (adopted from the blueprint)**
- Evidence status and legal status are separate fields:
  - **Evidence status:** Supported / Partly supported / Contradicted / Unsubstantiated / Unresolved.
  - **Legal status:** for example FIR, investigation, chargesheet, trial, convicted, acquitted, under appeal.
  - "Pending in court" is a legal status, never a truth verdict. This replaces the Verified / Disputed / Unsupported / Pending in court labels below.
- A **source registry**. Each source has a publisher, a class, method, coverage, licence, limitations, and date last checked. Every observation points to a source. The classes are:
  - A: primary record
  - B: independent dataset or survey
  - C: reproducible analysis
  - D: reporting
  - E: advocacy or incident tracker
  - F: remote sensing
- Non-government sources are welcome when the method is transparent and the result can be cross-checked. Never pick a source because it agrees with us. Different sources stay as separate lines and are never spliced into one series.
- Missing values carry a reason (not published, suppressed, not collected, not applicable). Boundaries carry a version (2014 Telangana, 2019 J&K and Ladakh, 2020 DNH-DD). Source snapshots are never overwritten.
- Every indicator page has a "What this does not prove" box.

**Pages (adopted from the blueprint)**
- The Government scorecard becomes a **project ledger**. Each scheme shows who proposed, sanctioned, funded, delivered and measured it, at centre, state and local level, across successive governments. Status steps: announced → approved → funded → under construction → operational → outcome measured → outcome independently evaluated.
- The Opposition record becomes a **legislative record** that covers every party in the same format.

**Design**
- No beige or paper background. Use a clean white base with cool grey surfaces, keeping Chakra navy as the primary colour. Saffron and green stay out of the palette, as before.

**Operations**
- Licence check for every map, photo and dataset before launch. The current development map uses an unlicensed shapefile.
- Hindi text needs human review before launch. It was first drafted by AI.
- Tooling: Anaconda on the build machine provides Python (data pipeline) and Node (conda envs `forge` and `pmtracker-build`), if a build step is needed later.

## Principles

The site is on India's side, not any party's: it shows wins and lags for every government since 1947, so no reader can dismiss it as a party page.

- Every number has a source link to the primary dataset (ministry dashboard, NCRB, RBI, NFHS, Census), plus an archived copy so a deleted page doesn't break the claim.
- Lags sit next to wins. Each indicator shows where India fell short (real wages, inequality, Manipur, paper leaks) beside what improved. That is what makes the wins credible.
- Same yardstick both ways. Compare like with like: per capita, per project, per lakh people, and against peer countries where that is the fair test.
- Facts separate from opinion. Data cards carry only sourced facts. Commentary lives in a clearly labelled "Perspective" layer that readers can switch off.
- Show what is unknown. When no data exists (dissent share of takedowns, bridge age at collapse), say so instead of filling the gap.
- No party colours, logos or leader photos on data views. People appear in the timeline as history, not as branding.
- Corrections are public. Every page shows its last change and links to a correction log.

Rules for the data layer:

- Store the source with every row: URL, archived copy, publisher, date pulled.
- Mark breaks in a series. NCRB and NFHS change definitions between rounds; charts show a break marker instead of joining the line.
- Never mix sources inside one line chart. Each line comes from one dataset.
- Check before you build: confirm each dashboard still publishes state-wise historical data and allows download.

## India map

Every map on the site uses India's official boundaries as published by the Survey of India; international basemaps (Google, Mapbox, OpenStreetMap defaults) show disputed lines and must not be used as-is.

- Whole of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, including Pakistan-occupied Kashmir, Gilgit-Baltistan and Aksai Chin, inside India's boundary.
- Arunachal Pradesh fully inside India.
- Current internal boundaries: the J&K and Ladakh UTs (2019), Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu merged (2020), Telangana (2014).
- Shapefile source: the Survey of India official political map. Community datasets such as DataMeet's are easier to use, but check every boundary against the Survey of India map before release.
- Historical layers (1857 to 1947) need their own basemap of British India and the princely states, labelled "historical", so they are never mistaken for today's map.
- Legal risk is real: platforms showing parts of J&K or Ladakh outside India have faced police complaints. Get the map checked once by someone who knows the current Survey of India guidelines before launch.
- Islands included: Andaman and Nicobar and Lakshadweep shown at every zoom, with an inset if needed.

## Hypothesis testing

Yes, worth doing: a "real change or random chance" label on each trend is the site's strongest defence against "you're cherry-picking". It only works if the same tests run on the bad numbers as on the good ones.

| Question | Test | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Did a yearly count really change between two periods? | Poisson or negative binomial rate ratio, with a 95% confidence interval | Bridge collapses 2004-13 vs 2014-24; riots; terror incidents |
| Did a trend bend when a policy started? | Interrupted time series (segmented regression) | Toilet coverage before and after Swachh Bharat (2014) |
| Did the policy cause it, or would it have happened anyway? | Difference-in-differences across states that adopted early vs late | Tap water in early vs late Jal Jeevan states |
| When did a series actually shift? | Change-point detection | Inflation, rupee volatility, NCRB categories |
| Is a gap between two groups bigger than chance? | Permutation test | State-wise outcomes by ruling party |

How to show it: a plain verdict on the chart ("Likely a real change", "Could be chance", "Not enough data"), with the interval and method one tap away.

Traps to build in:

- Reporting bias: older events were reported less (paper leaks, bridge collapses). Test shares and rates, not raw counts, and flag the bias on the chart.
- Definition changes: NCRB and NFHS categories shift between rounds. Test only within a consistent series.
- Causation: a significant change after 2014 doesn't prove the government caused it. Say "changed after", not "because of", unless a difference-in-differences design supports it.
- Cherry-picking tests: fix the list of tests and periods in advance and publish all results, including the ones that go against the government.

## Moderation and contributions

Anyone can submit; nothing goes live without a source and two approvals.

1. Submit: a logged-in user (phone OTP or Google) fills a form: claim, number, date, place, category, source URL. No source, no submission.
2. Auto-check: the system archives the source, checks the link works, and flags duplicates of existing entries.
3. Review: two reviewers check the number against the source. One must not be the person who invited the contributor.
4. Publish or reject: approved entries go live with the reviewers' initials; rejected ones get a reason the submitter can see.
5. Challenge: any visitor can flag an entry with a counter-source. Flags go back to review, and the outcome is logged publicly.

Roles: Contributor (submit), Reviewer (approve or reject), Editor (Perspective layer and page copy), Admin (users, roles, takedown requests).

Also needed: rate limits per user, a ban list, an audit log of every edit, and a public correction log.

## Sharing

Every fact card, chart and claim card has a share button that produces three things at once: an image, ready-to-paste text, and a short link back to the source.

- Image card: 1080x1080 for WhatsApp and Instagram, 1200x675 for X. Shows the claim, the number, a mini chart, the source name and the site link. Generated automatically, never hand-made.
- Copy text: under 280 characters for X and under 500 for Threads, plus a longer WhatsApp version. Always ends with the source and the link.
- Link previews: every card has its own URL with Open Graph tags, so pasting the link into WhatsApp, X or Facebook shows the card image.
- Languages: share text in English and Hindi at launch.
- No watermark wars: the site name sits small in a corner. The source is bigger than the brand.

## Tech, hosting, languages and legal

A stack you have used before keeps this buildable: a static front end with Supabase behind it.

| Part | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Front end | Static site (Svelte or plain JS with esbuild) | Fast on cheap phones, cheap to host |
| Maps | MapLibre GL with your own Survey of India GeoJSON/TopoJSON | No third-party basemap boundaries |
| Charts | D3 or Observable Plot | Full control over slider-driven redraws |
| Database, auth, moderation | Supabase (Postgres, phone OTP login, row-level security for roles) | Review queue and audit log in one place |
| Share images | Serverless image generation (Cloudflare Workers or Vercel OG) | Cards built from data, not by hand |
| Hosting | Cloudflare Pages or GitHub Pages, .in domain | Free tier, fast in India |

Non-negotiables:

- Phone first: under 1 MB on first load over 4G; maps load state shapes simplified.
- Languages: English and Hindi at launch; the data model stores every label in multiple languages from day one.
- Accessibility: every chart has a text summary and a data table one tap away.

Legal:

- User content makes the site an intermediary under India's IT Rules 2021: publish a grievance officer contact and act on valid complaints within the set deadlines.
- Personal data (phone numbers, emails) falls under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023: collect the minimum, state the purpose, allow deletion.
- Defamation: no claim about a named living person unless it comes from a court record, an official report or the person's own words, quoted with the source.
- Images: public-domain archive photos or your own; no news agency photos.

## Lessons from the Threads arguments

Each argument so far exposed a weak point that the site should handle by design.

| What happened in the threads | Site rule |
|---|---|
| The rupee fell more in % terms after 2014 than under UPA | Show the % fall honestly, beside volatility, forex reserves and peer currencies |
| Raw takedown counts made India look worst | Per-capita toggle and per-platform views; state plainly that no dissent share is published |
| Opponents quoted RSF and V-Dem ranks | A methodology note on every index: who is surveyed, how many, what is measured |
| Reported paper leaks rise after 2014 | Show the reporting-bias flag and the cancellation rate (about 1 in 6 before 2014, about 6 in 10 after) |
| Bridge collapses lacked age data | Use the NCRB trend; mark bridge age as "no official data" |
| Kandahar 1999, electoral bonds, Vyapam and the BSNL-Jio billing miss are real | Put them in the scorecard as lags. Owning them first stops the "you hide things" attack |
| A viral graphic put words in Mary Millben's mouth with no record of her saying them | A "Viral claim check" layer for quotes and graphics: found, not found, or altered |
| Gemini drew the wrong Bhai Parmanand | No AI-drawn portraits of real people; archive photos only, checked against a second source |
| Bhagat Singh was put on a right-wing list | Tag every historical figure with their own stated ideology and organisation |
| Sources were dismissed as biased (Newslaundry, Organiser) | Official data first; partisan outlets only to date an event, never for a number |
| Oil prices were compared across a 2020-22 window | Compare matching periods: crisis to crisis, calm to calm |

## What you may be missing

The biggest gaps are credit-splitting between centre and states, and who runs the site.

- Centre vs state credit: most schemes are co-funded and delivered by states. Show the funding split and the ruling party in each state, or both sides will claim each other's work.
- Opposition-run states that did well (for example, health or literacy records in some states) belong on the site too. It is the strongest proof that the site is for India, not a party.
- Funding disclosure: publish who pays for hosting and who the reviewers are, at least by role. Undisclosed money is the first attack on any political site.
- Your own exposure: decide whether you appear by name. Political sites draw harassment; an organisation name and a shared admin email protect you.
- Data downloads: CSV and an open API for every chart, so journalists and critics can check the numbers themselves.
- Embeds: let others embed a chart in their own posts, with the source attached.
- WhatsApp channel or newsletter that pushes a new fact card weekly.
- Red-team review: before launch, have two people from the other side try to break each page.
- Privacy-friendly analytics (Plausible or similar) instead of Google Analytics.
- Snapshots: freeze the data behind every shared card, so an old link always shows what it showed when shared.

## Build phases

Start with the page that answers most of your current arguments; add the history and the community features once that works.

1. Development tracker: official India map, state-wise year slider for tap water, toilets, electricity, roads and LPG, share cards. This alone covers most of what you argue.
2. Scorecard and myth vs record: wins and lags per scheme, plus the claim cards from your Threads arguments (rupee, takedowns, BSNL, China, paper leaks, bridges).
3. Issue rail and hypothesis tests: terror, corruption, communal violence and the rest, each with a per-government chart and a "real change or chance" verdict.
4. Current protests page: live allegations with Verified / Disputed / Unsupported / Pending in court labels and a dated update log.
5. Timeline since 1857: historical basemap, events, freedom fighters with accurate ideology tags.
6. Contributions and moderation: open submissions only after the review team exists.
7. Hindi and other languages, embeds, API.
